General Information: Diamond & CBN Tooling ▼

Diamond is pure carbon in a cubic structure — the hardest of all known materials. Diamond tools benefit many industrial applications, including grinding, turning, sawing, lapping and wear resistance, enabling faster operation, extended tool life, and accurate tolerances of shape and size. Diamond used in industry is either natural (extracted from the ground, crushed and shaped to suit a particular requirement) or synthetic (man-made for a specific application). Diamond is graded according to its size, shape and friability.

Diamond Applications — Water Based Soluble

Diamond is used for cutting or grinding hard, brittle and abrasive materials, but it is not suitable for grinding high carbon steel — iron has an affinity for carbon (which is what diamond is) at high temperature. Diamond tools are used for grinding hard or very abrasive materials, such as:

  • Tungsten Carbide
  • Fiberglass
  • Stone and Marble
  • Ferrite
  • Composite Materials
  • Non-Ferrous Metals
  • Industrial Ceramics
  • Rubber
  • Reinforced Plastics
  • Glass

CBN Applications — Oil Based Soluble

Cubic Boron Nitride (CBN) is man-made — a chemical bond of boron and nitrogen with a cubic lattice structure, produced under high temperature and pressure. Available in different grades and sizes, CBN is particularly designed for grinding hardened steels, and is second only to diamond in hardness. CBN is used for grinding high carbon steels (over 0.7%), such as:

  • High-Speed Steel
  • Stainless Steel
  • Tool Steel
  • Ball Bearing Steel
  • Die Steel
  • Nickel Super Alloys
  • Cobalt or Nickel-Based Hard High-Temperature Materials

Electro Plated Diamond Tools

Electro plating bonds Diamond/CBN particles to a steel blank using nickel as the bonding material. Electro plated tools cannot be dressed — the layer of nickel and Diamond/CBN follows exactly the given shape of the blank, which makes electro plated tools the best choice for profile grinding. They can also be re-plated: after the first use, the tool can be stripped and re-plated, giving an efficient and economic re-platable process.

Cold Vulcanising Adhesives & Conveyor Belt Repair

Londoco is now a UK distributor of Masso TRS cold vulcanising adhesives — rubber and PVC/PU adhesives, surface primers and rapid repair systems for splicing, patching, lagging and lining.

Masso TRS — available in the UK through Londoco
General Information: Cold Vulcanising Adhesives ▼

Cold vulcanising adhesives bond and repair conveyor belting at ambient temperature, without a hot vulcanising press. That makes them ideal for in-situ splicing, patching and repair where the belt cannot be removed from the line, and for lagging pulleys and drums. Most TRS adhesives are supplied as two-component systems: the adhesive is mixed with a hardener immediately before use and applied in the recommended number of coats, then left to cure.

Choosing the Right TRS Product

  • Mines & underground belts: TRS 2002 NEW
  • General rubber belt splicing & repair: TRS 4004
  • White / food-contact rubber belts: TRS 4004 WHITE
  • One-component, no hardener: TRS 5005
  • Large pulley & drum lagging, tank lining: TRS 3000 TL
  • Rapid belt repair, minimal downtime: TRS 6006
  • PVC / PU plastic belts: TRS PLASTO
  • Rubber-to-metal adhesion: TRS METAL PRIMER

Where Conveyor Belt Adhesives Are Used

Wherever conveyor belts run under abrasion, impact or sharp debris, fast and durable repairs reduce downtime and extend belt life. Typical UK users include:

  • Quarrying & aggregates
  • Waste management & recycling
  • Ports & bulk material handling
  • Mining & mineral processing
  • Cement & building materials
  • Food & agricultural processing
  • Airport baggage handling
  • Manufacturing, logistics & parcel sorting

Belt material matters: rubber belts are typically bonded with the polychloroprene (CR) adhesives, while PVC/PU belts require PU-based adhesives such as TRS PLASTO, or the TRS 6006 repair system used with the appropriate primer.
